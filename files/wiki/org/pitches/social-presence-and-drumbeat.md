# A weekly drumbeat: an email list, three channels and things people can share


> **Bet on and graduated, 2026-10-07.** The organiser bet on this pitch; it continues as [social-drumbeat.md](../prds/social-drumbeat.md). This page is kept as the pre-bet record.

Area pitch under [One goat feeds the town](one-goat-minimal-core-and-extensions.md).
Pre-bet, shaped in auto mode on 2026-10-07.

## Problem

A new conference starts unknown: "almost nobody knows your event exists",
and "one announcement is not a marketing plan"
(`sources/web/guides/rubyevents-how-to-organize-a-ruby-conference.md`,
§ Website & Marketing, § Keep giving people reasons). One organiser who
had run a conference said the fix is a new idea "every week... We have,
let's say, 40 weeks out. Every week counts" (same source, Irina
Nazarova). GoatConf has about 35 weeks between 2026-10-07 and early June
2027.

GoatConf's audience is wider than the guide's. It is "open,
multi-discipline": engineers, designers, product people, founders
(`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--first-answers.md`, question 2).
The guide's channel list is Ruby-shaped (Ruby Central Slack, Ruby
Discords, r/ruby, Ruby Weekly, ruby.social), and those are the
organiser's home ground ([the organiser](../organiser.md)), but they will
not reach a designer in Sarajevo or a founder in Zagreb.

## Appetite

**Small but steady:** about an hour a week from now to June, ideally owned
by one volunteer rather than the organiser (the guide's first volunteer
role is "social media and marketing", § What volunteers actually do).

## Solution

### Own the list, rent the channels

- **An email list from the first day.** "Social posts disappear but email
  gives you a way to announce the CFP... without hoping an algorithm feels
  generous" (guide, § Where to tell people). The registration tool or a
  plain signup on the website collects addresses with consent.
- **Three channels, chosen for reach:**
  - **LinkedIn**, where one organiser saw "a big bump of ticket sales"
    (guide, same section), and which 37.2% of Stack Overflow's 2025
    respondents used regularly
    (`sources/web/social/stackoverflow-survey-2025-community-platforms.md`;
    a sample biased toward Stack Overflow's own users).
  - **A community chat (Discord)** for attendees, speakers and extension
    hosts to find each other before the event: 38.9% in the same survey.
    It doubles as the place extension hosts coordinate.
  - **Bluesky or Mastodon** as a cheap third, for the open-source and
    Ruby crowd (Bluesky 10.8% in the survey; Mastodon was not asked).
- **Scheduling for free:** Buffer's free plan covers three channels with
  ten queued posts each (`sources/web/social/buffer-pricing.md`).
- **Borrowed reach:** Ruby newsletters and communities from the guide's
  list; regional tech meetups and communities in Bosnia and Herzegovina
  and its neighbours (names unknown, needs source); IPI Academy's own
  channels; and cross-promotion with other conferences, which the guide
  calls partners, not competition (guide, § Partner with other events).

### The drumbeat: a calendar of reasons

One public reason every week, planned in advance. A first draft of the
beats, to be dated once the event date is fixed:

- the date and venue announcement; the website goes live; registration
  and the email list open;
- why Tuzla: the goat story, the salt lake, what a week costs;
- the CFP opens; CFP reminders; the CFP closes;
- the extension slots open ("bring your meetup to GoatConf");
- each sponsor or supporter, thanked when they join;
- the reconfirmation call; speaker announcements one by one; the schedule;
- travel guides: how to get to Tuzla, where to stay, where to eat;
- the fringe calendar filling up; the activity day;
- last call; the week before; live from the day; thank-you and photos.

### Things people can share

Speaker cards, an "I'm attending" image, a copy-paste blurb and short city
clips, because "the best promotion usually does not come from the
conference account" (guide, § Give attendees something to share).
Templates come from [Design with impeccable](design-with-impeccable.md).

### A gap in the regional calendar

Balkan Ruby, the regional Ruby conference in Sofia, is skipping 2027:
"Balkan Ruby 2027 is off... We'll be back in 2028"
(`sources/web/funding/balkanruby-blog-2027-is-off.md`). For Ruby people
in the region, 2027 has one fewer reason to travel. GoatConf can say so
politely and invite them, and ask Balkan Ruby to share it.

## Rabbit holes

- **Being on every network.** Six half-tended accounts read worse than
  three active ones.
- **Paid ads.** They spend the organiser's money on the one thing
  personal invitations do better: "five personal invitations that turn
  into tickets matter" (guide, § Where to tell people).
- **Marketing only to Rubyists.** The organiser's network is the fastest
  start and the narrowest audience.

## No-gos

- No paid advertising from the organiser's 1,000 EUR.
- No buying or importing email lists; only people who signed up.
- No posting on behalf of sponsors beyond thanking them.

## Choices made in auto mode

- **LinkedIn, Discord and one open network.** Because the survey and the
  guide point there and three can be kept alive by one volunteer.
  Instagram is not chosen for 2027 because no source here measures it for
  this audience; it could be added if a volunteer owns it.
- **Email first.** Because it is the one channel GoatConf owns.
- **The calendar of reasons is in the pitch** so the weekly hour is spent
  posting, not deciding what to post.

## Open questions for the organiser

- Is there a volunteer who could own social from November?
- Which regional communities (Sarajevo, Tuzla, Zagreb, Belgrade,
  Ljubljana, Vienna) do you already know personally?
- Do you want your own LinkedIn and X presence to carry the first
  announcements?
