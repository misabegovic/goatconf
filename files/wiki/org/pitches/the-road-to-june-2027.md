## Problem

Each area pitch has its own timing, and they depend on each other: no
announcement before the venue is signed, no CFP without a website, no
sponsor sheet without a date. The guide's timings set the hard points:
lock date, venue and budget before announcing; sponsor budgets set "by
the previous November"; the CFP open "four to six months before the
event"; "most tickets are sold in the last weeks"
(`sources/web/guides/rubyevents-how-to-organize-a-ruby-conference.md`).
About eight months remain, inside the guide's "doable but stressful"
band (§ Give yourself a year).

## Rabbit holes

- **Slipping the October steps.** Every later date hangs on the venue
  agreement; if it slips past November, the CFP and the supporter asks
  slip with it.
- **Announcing before signing,** against the guide's first rule.

## No-gos

- No public announcement before the venue and dates are confirmed in
  writing.
- No CFP closing date extended after it is published.

## Choices made in auto mode

- **CFP from mid-December to late January.** Because it sits at the
  guide's six-month mark and leaves February for review, before
  speakers are announced in March.
- **Registration opens with the date, in November.** The organiser's
  choice (`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--coc-ledger-registration-cfp.md`);
  a spring reconfirmation keeps early free places from being forgotten.

## Open questions for the organiser

- Is mid-December too close to the holidays for opening a CFP, or a
  feature (people have time to write)?
