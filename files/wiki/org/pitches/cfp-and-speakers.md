# An open CFP for self-funded speakers, a few invited names, and a grant someone else pays for


> **Bet on and graduated, 2026-10-07.** The organiser bet on this pitch; it continues as [cfp-and-speakers.md](../prds/cfp-and-speakers.md). This page is kept as the pre-bet record.

Area pitch under [One goat feeds the town](one-goat-minimal-core-and-extensions.md).
Pre-bet, shaped in auto mode on 2026-10-07.

## Problem

The program is the one thing in the core attendees travel for: "You need
talks, because otherwise why would your boss let you go?"
(`sources/web/guides/rubyevents-how-to-organize-a-ruby-conference.md`,
Why a Conference). GoatConf has to fill one track with talks and
workshops from an open, multi-discipline field, while telling speakers
plainly that it cannot pay their way: "only apply if you can get your
travel costs covered, I don't cover that", with "like 100 euros per
speaker" under consideration, "maybe not even that"
(`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--conference-vision.md`).

Self-funded travel narrows the pool to speakers whose employers pay or
who can afford it ([speaker persona](../../../.claude/personas/users/speaker.md)).
The timing is tight too: the guide says to open the CFP four to six
months before the event (guide, § When to CFP), which for early June 2027
means between December 2026 and February 2027.

## Appetite

**Medium:** the CFP runs for a few weeks, but writing it, reaching out,
reviewing and assembling the program spans December to April, and
speaker care runs until the day.

## Solution

### What the CFP says

- **The program GoatConf wants,** written before proposals arrive: talks
  and workshops for an open, multi-discipline audience (engineering,
  design, product, founding), the length of a slot, who the audience is,
  the closing date, and that first-time speakers are welcome (guide,
  § How to run a CFP).
- **The money, honestly and first.** A sentence on the lines of Live!
  360's: "Speakers are responsible for their own travel costs and
  incidentals" (`sources/web/speakers/live360-2021-call-for-speakers.md`),
  followed by what GoatConf does give: a place on stage, a free
  registration, a clear answer to every logistical question, a speaker
  dinner if a supporter funds it, and the low cost of a stay in Tuzla.
  The guide's minimum is exactly that: "a free ticket, a clear answer to
  every logistical question before they have to ask it, and a response
  when they email you" (guide, § What speakers need).
- **Companies that pay a speaker's way are thanked as supporters.** The
  guide suggests that when a speaker's company pays travel, "offer to
  list their company as a sponsor" (same section). In the umbrella's
  terms that is a support extension, and it costs GoatConf nothing.

### A grant that is not the organiser's money

- **The 100 EUR contribution moves out of the core** and becomes a
  support extension: a sponsor funds a speaker pool, as EuRuKo 2024 sold
  "Speaker Travel & Accommodation" support from 500 EUR
  (`sources/web/funding/euruko-2024-sponsoring.md`).
- **Need-based, and invisible to reviewers.** PyCon US: "PyCon US does
  not want expenses to discourage you from submitting a proposal", and
  the grant checkbox "is not seen by the proposal reviewers and does not
  affect the review of your proposal"
  (`sources/web/speakers/pycon-us-2026-speaking-guidelines.md`). Write the
  Docs runs one sponsor-funded grant fund and does not name recipients
  (`sources/web/speakers/writethedocs-portland-2026-opportunity-grants.md`);
  EuroPython caps travel aid per person
  (`sources/web/speakers/europython-2026-financial-aid.md`).
- If no sponsor funds it, there is no grant, and the CFP says so.

### Invited names, then the open call

- **One or two invited speakers, both from the organiser's network,**
  confirmed before the CFP opens so it launches with a reason to submit
  (guide, § CFP or curated?). The rest of the three to four main-day talks
  come from the open call (organiser's choices,
  `sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--talks-team-and-site.md`). The
  network is the organiser's from EuRuKo 2024 and RubyConf Austria
  ([the organiser](../organiser.md)); since the audience is open and
  multi-discipline, the open call is where other disciplines get their
  slot.
- **Outreach for the open call:** the guide's Ruby channels; regional
  design, product and startup communities; IPI Academy's network; and
  RubyEvents' speaker directory for "recent speakers that were active in
  your geographic region" (guide, tip under § When to CFP).

### Review and assembly

- **A small committee** of three to five, with simple criteria (clarity,
  relevance, fit) and anonymised review, then the program assembled as a
  whole rather than by score (guide, § Build a CFP committee). Mixing
  disciplines on the committee matters because the audience is mixed.
- **The tool: a simple form,** on the organiser's choice
  (`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--coc-ledger-registration-cfp.md`).
  Tally is free with data stored in Europe
  (`sources/web/tooling/tally-pricing.md`). Proposals land in a shared
  sheet; for anonymised review, one person copies the proposals into a
  reviewers' sheet without names or bios, the step Sessionize and pretalx
  automate (`sources/web/tooling/sessionize-anonymous-mode.md`,
  `sources/web/tooling/pretalx-review-docs.md`). With three to four
  slots, a sheet is enough. Sessionize's community licence and pretalx
  stay noted for a larger edition.
- **Notify accepted speakers before announcing anyone,** tell declined
  speakers promptly, keep a short waitlist (guide, § When to CFP,
  § Accepted, declined, and waitlisted speakers).

### Speaker care

One clear acceptance email with everything (date, what GoatConf gives,
how to get to Tuzla and where to stay, deadlines); a check-in with
first-time speakers a few weeks out; a speaker dinner the night before if
a supporter pays for it (guide, § What speakers need, § Supporting
first-time speakers).

## Rabbit holes

- **Only local or only wealthy speakers.** Without a grant, the program
  may skew to people whose employers fly them. The grant extension is the
  answer, if funded.
- **Far more proposals than slots.** The main day has only three to four
  talks, by the organiser's choice
  (`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--freedom-square-and-few-talks.md`),
  and the invited names take some of them. Most good proposals will not
  fit; the CFP should say so, and offer the workshop day, hubs and fringe
  events as real places for them, not consolation.
- **Anonymity by hand.** Copying proposals without names is manual and
  easy to get wrong; one person owns it and nobody else sees the original
  sheet until review is done.

## No-gos

- No speaker travel paid from the organiser's 1,000 EUR.
- No paid speaking slots; sponsors do not buy talks (the charter's
  content-money separation).
- No public announcement of a speaker before they have confirmed.

## Choices made in auto mode

- **The 100 EUR becomes a sponsor-funded pool, not a core cost.** Because
  100 EUR times eight to twelve speakers would take most of the budget,
  and PyCon and EuroPython show the pool model works.
- **The tool question is settled by the organiser:** a simple form and a
  shared sheet.
- **Invited names before the CFP.** Because the guide says a confirmed
  name gives people a reason to submit, and the organiser has the network.
  The organiser confirmed: one or two invited, both from their network.

## Open questions for the organiser

- Who are the one or two invited speakers?
- Who could sit on a CFP committee with you, across disciplines?
- Talk length: 30 minutes, as in the guide's 30-30 format, or shorter?
