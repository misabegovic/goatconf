# Tuzla's history, the goat and the old town on foot

Agent-authored synthesis of what the sources say about the city's past,
its symbols and the walkable centre, as found on 2026-10-07. It describes
the city; it decides nothing. The practical page is
[Tuzla for GoatConf](../tuzla.md). Paths are under `sources/web/tuzla/`
unless written in full.

## A city named for salt

- **The name.** Tuzla comes from the Turkish *tuz*, salt
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`). The tourist board lists
  the older names, all about salt: Ad Salinas (Latin), Soli (South
  Slavic), Memleha-i Zir (Persian), Tuz (Turkish), and says the river
  Jala takes its name from the Greek for salt
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-history-of-tuzla.md`). Locals call it "the
  city on a grain of salt": hundreds of millions of tons of rock salt
  and salt water left by the Pannonian Sea (same source).
- **Older than Rome.** Neolithic pile dwellings stood in what is now the
  centre; two reconstructed salt-boiling pots date to about 5,000 and
  3,500 BC (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-archaeological-park.md`). The
  Bosnian Wikipedia dates the earliest salt working in Gornja Tuzla to
  about 5700 BC (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`). The first
  Neolithic finds came up in 1903 on what was then Appel Platz, today's
  Freedom Square (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-archaeological-park.md`).
- **Rome and Byzantium.** The salt museum page says the Romans called the
  place "To Salines" but that no material evidence of Roman salt
  extraction exists; the first written mention is Constantine
  Porphyrogennetos's *De administrando imperio*, which calls it Salenes
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-salt-museum.md`); the Bosnian Wikipedia dates
  that mention to 950 (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`). So
  "salt since Roman times" undersells it: salt since the Neolithic, with
  a Roman name and no Roman saltworks found.
- **Ottoman Tuzla.** The Ottomans took the area in 1460, lost it to
  Hungary in 1464 and took it back in 1474
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-salt-museum.md`). A salt well on today's Salt
  Square was dug or modernised in 1476 and up to 80 pans boiled brine
  there; Louis XIV bought Tuzla salt in the 17th century, and it was
  shown at the 1876 Philadelphia exhibition
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-history-of-tuzla.md`).
- **Austro-Hungarian Tuzla.** Austro-Hungarian troops entered the city on
  22 September 1878 after resistance (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`).
  The first industrial saltworks at Simin Han started on 25 March 1885,
  fed by a 3.5 km cast-iron brine pipe (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-salt-museum.md`);
  the Kreka coal mine opened the same year, and Donja Tuzla became simply
  "Tuzla" (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`). The brewery dates
  from 1884 (`sources/web/tuzla/pivara-tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`). Pumping
  brine made the ground sink: subsidence peaked in the 1970s and the
  city lost several thousand buildings
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-history-of-tuzla.md`). Firsts from the period:
  the first choral society in Bosnia and Herzegovina (1886) and its first
  theatre (17 April 1898) (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`).
- **Salt as heritage.** The old Kreka saltworks, a salt-well complex and
  an Ottoman salt well are a national monument and a Museum of Salt
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-salt-museum.md`). The Pannonian Lakes (2003
  onwards) were built partly to bring "a part of the Pannonian Sea" back
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-geological-exhibition-pannonica.md`).

## Miners and the Husino rebellion

- In December 1920, 7,000 miners from Tuzla, Breza and Zenica struck at
  Husino near Tuzla for higher wages; the strike became an armed
  rebellion put down by the army, and about 350 miners were tried in
  Tuzla in 1922 (`sources/web/tuzla/husino-rebellion-wikipedia.md`).
- In Yugoslavia 21 December was Miners' Day because of it; Ivan
  Sabolić's monument (1956) shows a miner dropping his pick for a rifle,
  and the folk song "Konjuh planinom" grew out of it (same source). The
  "Miner from Husino" statue stands in front of the Bosnian Cultural
  Center in town (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-monuments-and-attractions.md`).

## The 1990s and the Kapija

- Tuzla's 1990 vote went to the Social Democrats, the only municipality
  in Bosnia where the nationalist SDA did not take power; the city set up
  a multi-ethnic police and army force (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-massacre-wikipedia.md`).
  The city stayed outside the control of the JNA and VRS; 15 May 1992,
  the clash at Brčanska Malta, is marked as Tuzla's Defence Day
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`).
- **The Kapija massacre.** At 20:55 on 25 May 1995 a shell fired from VRS
  positions on Mount Ozren hit the Kapija, the centre of the city's
  nightlife, and killed 71 people (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-massacre-wikipedia.md`,
  `tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`). The wounded are counted as 240, 115 or 150
  depending on the source (same, and `tztz-kapija-memorial.md`). Only a
  few victims were over 30; the youngest, Sandro Kalesić, was two and a
  half (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-kapija-memorial.md`).
- **How it is remembered.** The dead are buried in the Slana Banja
  memorial complex; funerals were held at 4 a.m. to avoid shelling, and
  51 of the 71 lie together (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-massacre-wikipedia.md`,
  `tztz-kapija-memorial.md`). At the Kapija a memorial carries every name
  and the line "Ovdje se ne živi samo da bi se živjelo, ... Ovdje se i
  umire da bi se živjelo" (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-kapija-memorial.md`).
  The city runs a small memorial museum nearby
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-massacre-wikipedia.md`). The commander
  convicted for it, Novak Đukić, fled to Serbia, and denial of the
  massacre is a live political issue (same source). The anniversary falls
  about two weeks before GoatConf; any route through the Kapija (the goat
  hunt, a walking tour) should pass it quietly (suggestion).

## Tolerance and identity

- The Bosnian Wikipedia calls Tuzla "a city of great multicultural
  tradition" whose tolerance held through the Second World War and the
  1992 to 1995 war (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`). The
  organiser's 2023 city pitch made the same point with one view that
  holds a Catholic church, an Orthodox church and a mosque
  (`sources/web/tuzla/rubyevents-city-pitch-tuzla.md`).
- Thirteen national monuments sit in the city, among them the Orthodox
  Church of the Dormition, the Turali Bey (Poljska) Mosque, the Šarena
  (Atik, Behram Bey's) Mosque, the Hastahana (first public hospital) and
  the salt-production heritage; older mosques include Jalska (about 1600),
  Mejdan (1644) and Džindijska (17th century)
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-monuments-and-attractions.md`). The Catholic
  cemetery at Borić holds generations of the "Croats of Salt" (same
  source).
- **People.** Meša Selimović (1910 to 1982), author of *Death and the
  Dervish*, was born in Tuzla and taught at the gymnasium that has borne
  his name since 1989 (`sources/web/tuzla/mesa-selimovic-wikipedia.md`,
  `tztz-mesa-and-ismet-monument.md`). The painter Ismet Mujezinović
  (1907 to 1984) was born and died in Tuzla
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-atelier-ismet-mujezinovic.md`). Basketball
  player Mirza Delibašić and Razija Mujanović are named the best-known
  athletes; Jedinstvo's women won the European title in 1989
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`).

## The goat

- **The legend.** Under Austro-Hungarian rule a proclamation banned
  goats and ordered them killed to protect the forests; in Mosnik, a
  Tuzla neighbourhood, one man refused and kept a goat, and a visiting
  foreigner coined the line "Cijela Tuzla jednu kozu muzla" (all of Tuzla
  milked one goat). Some date the legend to the 10th century; Dragiša
  Trifković wrote it down in *Tuzlanski vremeplov*
  (`sources/web/tuzla/mondo-tuzlanska-koza-legenda.md`). The organiser's
  own telling (EuRuKo 2023): goats were banned, one family hid one, and
  that goat fed the city with milk and cheese
  (`sources/web/tuzla/rubyevents-city-pitch-tuzla.md`).
- **The statue.** One press source puts the goat statue in front of
  Hotel Tuzla (`sources/web/tuzla/mondo-tuzlanska-koza-legenda.md`),
  which is at Ul. ZAVNOBiH-a 13 (`sources/web/tuzla/cvent-hotel-tuzla-address.md`).
  The exact spot is (unverified, 2026-10-07). Someone should photograph
  it before the goat hunt is designed.
- **The goat as a symbol.** The tourist board's city guide makes
  "Tuzla's goat" a photo stop and says the legends made it into famous
  songs (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-city-guide-tztz-2025.md`); the goat
  appears on postcards beside the salt wells and the Čaršijska česma
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`); the local basketball
  club's hall carries a goat (`sources/web/tuzla/rubyevents-city-pitch-tuzla.md`).

## The old town on foot

Everything below is within the pedestrian centre the free city Wi-Fi
covers: Salt Square, Freedom Square, the Korzo (Turali Bey's Street from
the Kapija to the statues of Meša and Ismet), the City Park, Trgovačka
and Gradska streets and the middle of Slana Banja
(`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-wireless-city.md`).

- **Freedom Square (Trg slobode).** About 8,000 m², called the largest
  square in Bosnia and Herzegovina; its centre is a large fountain shaped
  like a salt-boiling dish, with a stećak-style sculpture on the north
  side; around it stand the 16th-century Bazaar Mosque, the Ruždija
  (1883), the Čaršijska česma drinking fountain (1888) and the Baroque
  building (1900) (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-square-of-freedom.md`). The
  Baroque building was renewed in 2009 (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-monuments-and-attractions.md`).
  The česma is in pseudo-Moorish style by architect Franz Mihanović
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`). This is where GoatConf's
  breaks happen.
- **Salt Square (Solni trg).** An open-air salt museum: an Ottoman salt
  well, a fountain given by Ravenna in the shape of a Neolithic salt dish
  with a mosaic, and a replica of how salt was boiled
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-salt-square.md`, `tztz-salt-museum.md`).
- **The Korzo and "kod Meše i Ismeta".** The statues of Selimović and
  Mujezinović on the Korzo are the city's meeting point: "at Meša and
  Ismet" is enough (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-mesa-and-ismet-monument.md`).
  The Tourist Information Center, opened on 1 June 2023, is at
  Turalibegova 5 beside the Korzo (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-tourist-information-center.md`).
- **The Kapija** memorial, at one end of the Korzo (above).
- **Places of worship.** Turali Bey's Mosque, a national monument, is on
  the tourist train's route (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-tourist-train.md`);
  the Franciscan church of Sts Peter and Paul, a 20th-century building
  with a gallery inside, is at Franjevačka 26
  (`sources/web/tuzla/inyourpocket-franciscan-church-tuzla.md`). The
  Orthodox Church of the Dormition is a national monument
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-monuments-and-attractions.md`); its address and
  visiting hours are (unknown, needs source).
- **City Park.** Granite paths, free Wi-Fi, a statue of King Tvrtko I
  Kotromanić and an obelisk with the Charter of Kulin Ban; a pedestrian
  bridge links it to the Pannonian Lakes (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-city-park.md`).
- **Museums.** The Museum of Eastern Bosnia holds about 30,000 objects,
  some over 7,000 years old, including a relief of Mithras found in 1966
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-museum-of-eastern-bosnia.md`). The
  International Portrait Gallery (1964) holds more than 5,000 works; two
  of its collections are national monuments
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-international-portrait-gallery.md`). The
  Ismet Mujezinović Atelier, the painter's house, runs six-month artist
  residencies and its garden in the centre hosts concerts, exhibitions
  and book launches (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-atelier-ismet-mujezinovic.md`),
  a possible extension venue. At the lakes, the Pannonica geological
  exhibition shows 100 minerals and 120 fossils, including tuzlaite, a
  mineral found only in Tuzla's salt (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-geological-exhibition-pannonica.md`),
  and the archaeological park of pile dwellings opened in summer 2006
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-archaeological-park.md`). Opening hours and
  prices are (unknown, needs source).
- **Central market.** Open since 1952 in the centre, with fruit,
  vegetables and local products (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-central-market.md`).
- **The Jala** rises on Majevica and runs 37 km to the Spreča
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia-bs.md`).
- **Ilinčica** is one of the hills, with Kicelj and Gradina, where the
  city's residential areas lie (`sources/web/tuzla/tuzla-wikipedia.md`);
  what there is to see there is (unknown, needs source).

### Getting around the centre

- **Tourist train** from Freedom Square to the lakes, run by the city's
  transport company, on workdays except Mondays; departures from the
  lakes at 11:00, 12:00, 13:00, 14:00, 15:00 and 16:00 (as published,
  undated) (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-tourist-train.md`).
- **Nextbike** bike share: 20 KM a year to register, the first 30
  minutes free, then 1.5 KM an hour (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-nextbike.md`).
- **Free Wi-Fi** "Tuzla Wireless City", 30-minute sessions that can be
  renewed (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-wireless-city.md`).
- **Accessibility** of the old town for wheelchairs and prams is
  (unknown, needs source); the park paths are granite-paved
  (`sources/web/tuzla/tztz-city-park.md`).
