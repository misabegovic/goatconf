Muhamed Isabegović (Meister Labs, Ruby developer) delivers a city pitch at EuRuKo 2023 proposing Tuzla, Bosnia and Herzegovina, as a host city for a future EuRuKo. He frames Tuzla as an appealing, diversity-friendly, and cost-effective alternative to typical Western and Northern European conference locations, emphasizing culture, community impact, and unique venue options.

Why Tuzla: The speaker challenges attendees to consider Bosnia as a destination for both travel and conferences, highlighting the opportunity to experience a new region while engaging in the Ruby community. Tuzla is described as notably tolerant and diversity-friendly, exemplified by the close proximity of a Catholic church, an Orthodox church, and a mosque.

City profile: Tuzla is nicknamed the “Salt Lake City” due to its artificial salt lakes. It is the third-largest city in Bosnia and is approaching its 1,074th anniversary next year, underscoring its historical depth. The city is portrayed as green and welcoming.

Timing and venues: The artificial salt lakes are open through August 15; the proposed conference timing is the first two weeks of August to allow swimming by day and lakeside networking events by night (already scouted by the speaker). Confirmed venue options include a luxury hotel for a smaller conference or the city’s sports hall (home to the local basketball team, featuring a goat emblem) for a large event.

Partnerships and sponsorships: The pitch notes existing partners and sponsors, including a local NGO (Futures Foundation) experienced in event organization. A potential sponsorship with Goat Simulator is under negotiation, aligning with its 10-year anniversary and a Bosnian founder.

Hospitality and affordability: Tuzla offers diverse cuisine with vegetarian and vegan options. The local brewery is celebrating its 140th anniversary, and the speaker cites very affordable beer prices (about 0.7 EUR for 0.5L). Additional social venues include the central town square (with nearby pubs/restaurants) and “Mother’s Lake,” the city’s water source, with options for gatherings and parties.

Examples and Illustrations

Cultural diversity visual: A single vista where a Catholic church, Orthodox church, and mosque are visible underscores Tuzla’s interfaith tolerance.

Goat statue anecdote: During the Austrian Empire’s occupation, goats were banned; one Tuzla family hid a goat that fed the city with milk and cheese. A statue commemorates this. The speaker parallels this with EuRuKo potentially being “the goat” that nourishes Bosnia’s Ruby community, which has never hosted a Ruby conference.
