# Supporters who do something, and hiring with consent instead of an agency


> **Bet on and graduated, 2026-10-07.** The organiser bet on this pitch; it continues as [supporters-and-hiring.md](../prds/supporters-and-hiring.md). This page is kept as the pre-bet record.

Area pitch under [One goat feeds the town](one-goat-minimal-core-and-extensions.md).
Pre-bet, shaped in auto mode on 2026-10-07.

## Problem

GoatConf's core fits the organiser's 1,000 EUR, but every stretch goal
(coffee, video, a speaker grant, a party) needs someone else's money. The
organiser floated a way in: "If companies want to hire these folks they
can reach out and help the event in that way, as a sort of agency
maybe... Idk" (`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--conference-vision.md`).

Two things pull against each other. Hiring companies are the guide's
clearest sponsors: "If they make one hire from people they met at your
conference and that hire would have otherwise cost $20,000 in recruiter
fees, the $3,000 sponsorship paid for itself several times over"
(`sources/web/guides/rubyevents-how-to-organize-a-ruby-conference.md`,
§ What sponsors want). But a free event that feels like a recruiting
funnel loses the people it is for
([attendee persona](../../../.claude/personas/users/attendee.md)). And
the calendar is against a first edition: sponsor budgets are often set
"by the previous November for the following year" (guide, § Lower your
expectations), weeks from now.

## Appetite

**Medium:** a sponsor sheet in November 2026, personal outreach through
the winter, a job board and a hiring corner set up by spring.

## Solution

### Supporters, not tiers of logos

- **Support is an extension slot.** A supporter pays for one named thing
  from a menu of stretch goals: coffee for a day, lunch vouchers, the
  speaker grant pool, video recording, the after-party, the activity day,
  badges and lanyards. It is thanked for exactly that, by name, and it
  pays the supplier of that item directly or gives it in kind: no money
  goes through the organiser (`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--no-money-through-me.md`). The guide
  values sponsors who want to do something over logo placement (guide,
  § Beyond the logo), and Balkan Ruby already sells named items, Food and
  Coffee at 1,500 EUR each beside tiers of 500, 1,500 and 2,500 EUR
  (`sources/web/funding/balkanruby-sponsorship.md`).
- **A small entry price for small supporters.** Balkan Ruby's Mini Ruby
  tier starts at 500 EUR; EuRuKo 2024 had "Anonymous support" from 500 EUR
  (same source; `sources/web/funding/euruko-2024-sponsoring.md`). GoatConf
  can go lower for individuals and local companies, since Tuzla prices
  are lower. The amounts are set in the budget pitch.
- **Fewer, committed supporters** over many small ones, because each is a
  relationship to manage (guide, § Package design).
- **Thanks are capped and content is not for sale.** FOSDEM limits
  sponsor thanks and keeps content and funding apart in its charter
  (`sources/web/funding/fosdem-charter.md`); GoatConf's charter takes the
  same line.
- **Who to ask first:** companies with a personal connection to the
  organiser ("You're selling you", guide, § Lower your expectations); the
  sponsors of EuRuKo 2024 and RubyConf Austria; companies hiring in
  Tuzla and the region; IPI Academy's employer partners, which it
  describes as "a number of collaborations with employers"
  (`sources/web/venue/ipi-akademija-about.md`); and local foundations, as
  the BH Futures Foundation paid for scholars to attend DevFest Sarajevo
  2025 (`sources/web/funding/bhff-devfest-sarajevo-2025.md`).

### Hiring with consent

The organiser's "agency" idea, reshaped so attendees choose to take part:

- **A job board** on the website, one entry per supporting company that
  wants it, as EuRuKo 2024 ran
  (`sources/web/hiring/euruko-2024-job-board.md`) and sold to hiring firms
  as cheaper than a headhunter, who "are looking for half a years'
  salary" (`sources/web/funding/euruko-2024-sponsoring.md`).
- **A hiring corner or office hours** at the venue, where companies meet
  people who walk up.
- **An opt-in "open to work" signal.** At registration, an attendee may
  choose to be visible to hiring partners. Nobody else is. "We do not
  share any attendee information with sponsors"
  (`sources/web/funding/writethedocs-portland-2026-prospectus.md`); Strange
  Loop's policy is never to share or sell
  (`sources/web/hiring/strangeloop-privacy-policy.md`); LeadDev makes
  badge scanning the attendee's choice and says "Choosing not to have
  your badge scanned will not affect your participation in the event"
  (`sources/web/hiring/leaddev-london-terms.md`).

### What "agency" would mean, and why not in 2027

WeAreDevelopers grew from a congress into a hiring platform that charges
a share of a hire's first-year salary
(`sources/web/hiring/wbs-wearedevelopers-ben-ruschin.md`, figures from
2017 to 2019). That is the full version of the organiser's idea, and it
is a business, not a free community event: it needs a company, contracts,
placement liability and data handling. This pitch keeps the door open (a
later edition could pilot introductions for a fee to the event) and does
not walk through it in 2027.

## Rabbit holes

- **Supporters used to paying an event.** Most sponsors expect to pay
  the conference an invoice. GoatConf takes no money: "No money goes
  though me" (`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--no-money-through-me.md`).
  Each menu item therefore names its supplier, and the supporter pays
  that supplier or gives in kind; the public ledger in the
  [budget pitch](budget-a-goat-and-a-menu.md) shows it.
- **Supporters wanting more than thanks:** a talk, the attendee list, a
  keynote. The charter says no to all three.
- **Late start.** A first edition announced in winter will hear "not this
  year" often (guide, § Lower your expectations). Small, local and
  personal supporters are the realistic base.

## No-gos

- No attendee list sold or given to anyone.
- No sponsored talks.
- No placement fees in 2027.
- No money from a supporter to the organiser or to GoatConf; supporters
  pay suppliers or give in kind, and every line is public.

## Choices made in auto mode

- **A menu of named items over metal tiers.** Because it is the
  guide's "beyond the logo" advice and the minimal-core idea in money
  form: each item is one stretch goal, funded or not.
- **Opt-in visibility over badge scanning.** Because a free event can
  avoid the hardware, and an opt-in list is easier to explain.
- **Agency deferred.** Because it changes what GoatConf is, legally and in
  spirit, and the first edition needs trust more than revenue.

## Open questions for the organiser

- Which companies would you ask first, personally?
- Should the job board be open to any company, or only supporters?
