---
title: "Hosts who fund and organise an accepted event are listed as supporters, if they agree"
date: 2026-10-18
kind: conversation
---

After the BH Futures Foundation was listed as a supporter for funding
and organising its meetup
(`sources/conversations/2026-10-18--org--bhff-funding-and-organising.md`),
the agent said it had treated that as one listing, and that making it a
general rule would change the supporters page and the charter. It also
asked whether to fix Meister's thank-you card, which reads "for funding
christoph Lipautz's travel" because the card lowercases the first letter
of every line. The organiser wrote, verbatim:

> Yes, we should list these as supporters also

The agent drafted an amendment to the supporters decision and the public
wording, and asked four questions. The organiser chose:

- **Who:** "Any host, people too". A person, group or company that funds
  and organises an accepted event may be listed, not only organisations.
- **Opt-in:** "If they agree". A host is listed once it agrees and gives
  or approves a logo, as the foundation did; nothing is automatic.
- **Meister's card:** "Yes, fix it too". The card stops lowercasing the
  first letter of a line, and Meister's two cards are rendered again.
- **The wording:** "Approve as drafted", with "an organisation" read as
  "a host" after the first answer.

The rule, as approved: a host that funds and organises an event the
organiser accepted as part of GoatConf 2027 is listed on the site as a
supporter, the same as online supporters, with its logo and link, if it
agrees. No money goes through the organiser, so there is nothing to
invoice. Its line names the event. Being listed buys nothing: no talk,
no stage time, no say over the program and nothing at GoatConf's venue.
The supporters page, the charter, the propose an event page, the
supporters README, the README and the summary in `llms.txt` say so. The
foundation's listing now falls under this rule.
