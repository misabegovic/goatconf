# A weekly drumbeat: an email list, three channels and things people can share

Product requirements for GoatConf's public voice from now to June 2027.
It graduates the pitch
[A weekly drumbeat](../pitches/social-presence-and-drumbeat.md), on which
the organiser bet on 2026-10-07
(`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--bet-on-every-pitch.md`), and sits
under the epic [GoatConf 2027](../epics/goatconf-2027.md).

## Objective

**One planned public reason to care about GoatConf every week until June
2027, carried by an email list GoatConf owns and three channels one
volunteer can keep alive, so that people outside the organiser's own
network hear about the event in time to come.**

## Background

A new conference starts unknown: "almost nobody knows your event exists",
and "one announcement is not a marketing plan"
(`sources/web/guides/rubyevents-how-to-organize-a-ruby-conference.md`,
§ Website & Marketing). One organiser quoted in the guide put the fix as a
new idea "every week... Every week counts" (same source). From 2026-10-07
to early June 2027 GoatConf has about 35 weeks.

The audience is "open, multi-discipline": engineers, designers, product
people, founders (`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--first-answers.md`,
question 2), and the event is in English (same source, question 7). The
organiser's own reach is the Ruby community, from EuRuKo 2024 and RubyConf
Austria ([the organiser](../organiser.md)). Ruby channels are the fastest
start and the narrowest audience, so the drumbeat has to borrow reach
beyond them.

One regional fact makes 2027 a good year to ask: Balkan Ruby, the regional
Ruby conference in Sofia, says "Balkan Ruby 2027 is off... We'll be back in
2028" (`sources/web/funding/balkanruby-blog-2027-is-off.md`).

The repository will be public
(`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--goatconf-will-be-public.md`), and
anything that reaches the public, a social post or an announcement
included, is the organiser's decision; an agent's deliverable is the draft
(`AGENTS.md`, § Public acts are the operator's). The drumbeat is shaped
around that: agents and the volunteer prepare, the organiser says go.

## Affected personas

- **[Attendee](../../../.claude/personas/users/attendee.md).** *Now:* there
  is nothing to hear about GoatConf. *Target:* they learn of it early
  enough to plan a trip, can sign up for email without registering, and
  get the practical news (date, registration, travel) in their inbox
  rather than hoping a post reaches them.
- **[Speaker](../../../.claude/personas/users/speaker.md).** *Now:* no
  call is announced. *Target:* the CFP's opening, reminders and closing
  reach them through the list and the channels, and once accepted they get
  a card to share with their own audience.
- **[Extension host](../../../.claude/personas/users/extension-host.md).**
  *Now:* nobody knows slots exist. *Target:* a "bring your meetup to
  GoatConf" beat tells them how to add an extension, and a community chat
  gives them a place to coordinate with other hosts.

Secondary:

- **[Hiring partner](../../../.claude/personas/users/hiring-partner.md)**:
  is thanked by name when they join, which is the only posting done about
  supporters.
- **[Volunteer](../../../.claude/personas/users/volunteer.md)**: one of
  them owns the weekly hour, if one can be found.

## Now / Perceived / Target

- **Now (2026-10-07):** no email list, no GoatConf account on any network,
  no calendar of posts, no shareable assets. No date is confirmed, and
  registration opens only when it is
  (`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--coc-ledger-registration-cfp.md`,
  question 3). The site will live at muhamed.at/goatconf
  (`sources/conversations/2026-10-07--org--no-food-or-drinks-and-site-path.md`).
- **Perceived:** (unknown, needs source) what the organiser expects their
  own network to bring is not recorded. No source measures how many people
  outside the Ruby community the organiser can reach, or which regional
  communities they know personally; the risk is that a Ruby-shaped reach
  is taken for the multi-discipline audience the event wants.
- **Target:** an email list collecting addresses with consent from the day
  the site goes live; three channels posting at least weekly; a dated
  calendar of reasons in this repository; shareable templates for
  speakers and attendees; and borrowed reach from regional communities,
  IPI Academy's channels and other conferences.

## Scope

GoatConf owns one channel, an email list, and rents three others: LinkedIn,
a community chat (Discord) for attendees, speakers and extension hosts, and
one open network (Bluesky or Mastodon). The channel choice rests on the
guide's advice and on Stack Overflow's 2025 survey, where 37.2% of
respondents used LinkedIn and 38.9% Discord regularly and Bluesky stood at
10.8% (`sources/web/social/stackoverflow-survey-2025-community-platforms.md`,
a sample biased toward Stack Overflow's own users). Posts are queued with a
free scheduler; Buffer's free plan covers three channels with ten queued
posts each (`sources/web/social/buffer-pricing.md`).

The core of the work is a calendar of reasons kept in this repository: one
beat a week, drafted ahead and dated once IPI confirms the date. The first
draft of the beats, carried over from the pitch, is:

- the date and venue; the website live; registration and the email list
  open;
- why Tuzla: the goat story, the salt lake, what a week costs;
- the CFP opens, reminders, the CFP closes;
- the extension slots open;
- each supporter thanked when they join;
- speaker announcements one by one; the schedule;
- travel: how to get to Tuzla, where to stay, where to eat;
- the fringe calendar filling up; the activity day;
- last call; the week before; live from the day; thank-you and photos.

GoatConf also gives people things to share, because "the best promotion
usually does not come from the conference account" (guide, § Give
attendees something to share): speaker cards, an "I'm attending" image, a
copy-paste blurb and short city clips, built from the templates of
[Design with impeccable](design-with-impeccable.md). Borrowed reach comes
from the guide's Ruby newsletters and communities, regional tech
communities (names unknown, needs source), IPI Academy's own channels, and
cross-promotion with other conferences, including a polite invitation to
Balkan Ruby's audience.

## No-gos

- No paid advertising, from the organiser's fallback money or anyone's.
- No bought or imported email lists; only people who signed up themselves.
- No email addresses or other subscriber data in this repository, which
  will be public.
- No posting on behalf of supporters beyond thanking them for what they
  fund.
- No post, email or announcement published without the organiser's go;
  agents draft, the organiser (or a volunteer the organiser has cleared
  for that beat) publishes.
- No more than three rented channels for 2027.

## Rabbit holes

- **Being on every network.** Six half-tended accounts read worse than
  three active ones. Instagram or X are added only if a volunteer owns
  them.
- **Marketing only to Rubyists.** The organiser's network is the fastest
  start and the narrowest audience; the calendar must carry beats aimed at
  design, product and founder communities.
- **Perfect assets before the first post.** A plain announcement on time
  beats a polished one late; templates improve as the design work lands.
- **Running a newsletter product.** The list exists to announce; it is not
  a content publication with its own editorial cadence.

## Appetite

**Small but steady:** about an hour a week from November 2026 to June 2027,
ideally owned by one volunteer rather than the organiser, since the guide
names "social media and marketing" as a first volunteer role
(guide, § What volunteers actually do). The setup (accounts, list, the
calendar file, first templates) fits a few evenings. If the weekly hour
turns into several, the channel count drops before the cadence does.

## Success metrics

- **A beat published every week from the date announcement to the event.**
  Observed by counting dated entries marked published in the calendar file
  in this repository against the number of weeks, checked monthly.
- **An email list of at least 300 people by 1 May 2027** (more than the
  hall's cap of about 120, since not everyone who subscribes comes).
  Observed as the subscriber count in the list tool's export on that date,
  recorded as a number only.
- **At least a quarter of registrations from outside the Ruby community.**
  Observed only if the registration form asks how people heard of
  GoatConf or what they work on; counted from the registration tool's
  export at registration close. If the form does not ask, this metric is
  recorded as not checked.
- **At least three external communities or conferences share an
  announcement.** Observed as a list of links in the calendar file, counted
  the week before the event.

- Which regional communities (Sarajevo, Tuzla, Zagreb, Belgrade,
  Ljubljana, Vienna) does the organiser know personally?
- Should the organiser's own LinkedIn and X presence carry the first
  announcements, before GoatConf accounts have followers?
- Bluesky or Mastodon as the open network?
- Who besides the organiser may publish a beat, and does the organiser
  clear each beat or the calendar as a whole?

## Decision needed

**Where the email list lives and how consent is recorded:** the
registration tool, the same form tool as the CFP, or a dedicated newsletter
service, given that subscriber data must never enter this public
repository and that people must be able to subscribe before registration
opens. The ADR also records the shape of the calendar file in this
repository (one dated entry per beat, its channels, its draft and its
published state) and who is cleared to publish.
