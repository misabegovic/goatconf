# The RubyEvents conference guide, read for GoatConf

*How to Organize a Ruby Conference* (guides.rubyevents.org, MIT, Hans
Schnedlitz) is a practical guide for a first regional conference of
100 to 200 people, drawn from organisers of Rocky Mountain Ruby, Brighton
Ruby, Blue Ridge Ruby, Friendly.rb, RubyConf Austria and others. The
organiser pointed at it on 2026-10-07. This page lists what it says,
section by section, and what that means for GoatConf, whose facts are on
[GoatConf 2027: what is known](../goatconf-2027.md). Quotes are from the
snapshot (`sources/web/guides/rubyevents-how-to-organize-a-ruby-conference.md`).

GoatConf differs from the guide's reader in three ways, and every reading
below accounts for them: it is **free**, so there is no ticket revenue to
cover a base budget; it targets **120 to 300**, at and above the guide's
upper bound; and its audience is **open and multi-discipline**, not Ruby
only, though the organiser's Ruby network is its first reach.

## The rules that change the plan

1. **Lock date, venue and rough budget before announcing.** "Not just
   thought about, actually decided and signed." For GoatConf: the week
   (first or second of June 2027) and a written agreement with IPI
   Academy come before any public announcement.
2. **Give yourself a year.** "Six months is doable but stressful." From
   2026-10-07 to early June 2027 is about eight months: inside the
   stressful band, so the plan must be minimal by necessity as well as by
   choice.
3. **Sponsor budgets are set early,** "sometimes by the previous November
   for the following year". For a June 2027 event that means a sponsor
   sheet should exist in November 2026, weeks from now, or most 2027
   budgets are gone.
4. **A base budget plus stretch goals.** The base is the minimum that
   makes the event happen; t-shirts, breakfast and video unlock only if
   money arrives. With no tickets, GoatConf's base must fit inside the
   organiser's 1,000 EUR plus what the host gives in kind, and everything
   else is a stretch goal a sponsor or an extension host can fund. This
   is the guide's own mechanism, and it is GoatConf's "minimal core,
   others extend" in money terms.
5. **Open the CFP four to six months out.** For early June 2027: open
   between December 2026 and February 2027, keep it open a few weeks,
   notify accepted speakers before announcing anyone.

## Format and identity

- **Single track** for 100 to 200 people, so everyone shares one frame of
  reference. GoatConf at up to 300 can stay single track in one hall if
  the hall holds it; workshops are the natural second room.
- **One or two days**; "for a first conference, one day is the safer
  choice". Left open by the organiser; the format pitch weighs it.
- **A clear identity tied to the place:** Blue Ridge Ruby's river tubing,
  Friendly.rb's walking tours and mountain day trip on the third day,
  RubyConf Austria's classical music. For GoatConf: Tuzla, its salt lake,
  and its low cost are the identity, not decoration.

## Website and marketing

- A simple site as soon as date and venue exist: when, where, who it is
  for, why come; the code of conduct early; travel basics (nearest
  airport, where to stay); an **accessibility** section that lists what is
  and is not present.
- The site also serves sponsors (credibility), speakers (worth
  submitting?) and employees asking a manager.
- Sell the trip before the lineup: the city, the venue, food, the
  after-party.
- A **weekly drumbeat** of announcements, an **email list** from day one,
  and **shareable material** (speaker cards, "I'm attending" images, city
  clips).
- Channels listed: Ruby Central Slack, Ruby and Ruby Europe Discords,
  r/ruby and r/rails, Ruby Weekly, Short Ruby Newsletter, RubyFlow,
  LinkedIn (a "big bump" for one organiser), X, Bluesky, Mastodon
  (ruby.social). GoatConf's open audience needs channels beyond these.

## Money

- First conferences lose money or break even; that is normal.
- **Skip hotel room blocks and catering:** the two expensive mistakes. An
  open two-hour lunch with a restaurant list works and is cheap, which
  suits Tuzla.
- **Video is a stretch goal**, not a baseline ("the people who watch
  conference videos on YouTube rounds to zero", Andy Croll).
- Ticketing tools named: Tito (the Ruby default), Luma, pretix (used by
  Baltic Ruby). GoatConf needs them only for free registration.

## Speakers

- A CFP opens the door to unknown speakers and signals fairness; invited
  keynotes are approached before the CFP opens so it launches with a
  name.
- Decide the program you want before proposals arrive and publish it:
  length, audience, deadline, first-timers welcome.
- A **CFP committee** with simple criteria (clarity, relevance, fit),
  optionally anonymised; assemble the program as a whole, not by score.
- **Be honest upfront if travel is not covered.** The minimum for speakers
  is a free ticket, clear logistics and replies. An honorarium is "a
  gesture of respect"; speakers whose company pays can be listed as a
  sponsor instead. This matches the organiser's self-funded rule and the
  100 EUR idea.
- A **speaker dinner** the night before; a **waitlist**; extra support
  for first-time speakers.
- RubyEvents' speaker directory finds speakers active in a region.

## The program

- **The 30-30 format:** 30 minutes of talk, 30 of break; six to eight
  talks fill a day with real conversation time.
- **Pacing:** the densest talk first, something lighter after lunch, a
  closer that sticks; spread first-time speakers.
- **Beyond the talks:** "Post-conference activities, a karaoke night, a
  hike, a day trip, work especially well." The guide names the
  organiser's own city-wide treasure hunt in Vienna at RubyConf Austria.
- A prepared **MC**, and ten minutes at the start explaining the **code
  of conduct**.

## Sponsors and partnerships

- Sponsors most likely to say yes to a first event have a personal
  connection; "you're selling you". The organiser's EuRuKo 2024 and
  RubyConf Austria record is the asset.
- Three kinds: **hiring** sponsors (one hire that would have cost
  20,000 USD in recruiter fees pays for a 3,000 USD sponsorship), tools
  and infrastructure (brand), and community sponsors (they want it to
  exist). The hiring case is the guide's version of the organiser's
  "sort of agency" idea.
- Fewer sponsors at higher amounts; flexible tiers; and sponsors who
  *do* something (a game night, an after-party) are worth more than a
  logo. That is an extension slot in all but name.
- **Other events are partners:** cross-promotion, traded tickets, the
  **Ruby Triathlon** (three conferences in a row; 2026 ran Rubycon Italy,
  Balkan Ruby in Sofia, RubyConf AT in Vienna). Whether a 2027 triathlon
  could include Tuzla is (unknown, needs source).

## Volunteers, the day, after

- Volunteers each own a domain (social, catering, design and swag,
  speakers and CFP, MC); a regular call before the event; find a
  **co-organiser**.
- On the day, registration, speakers and catering each have an owner, so
  the organiser sees the whole room. Have someone to call when things go
  wrong.
- Afterwards: settle finances within two weeks, a feedback survey within a
  week, no rush on video.
- **Sustainability:** a legal entity, a bank account in its name,
  documented processes, and successors grown from volunteers. "You do not
  legally need a company", but it helps a recurring event; for a free
  event taking sponsor money, who invoices sponsors is a real question
  (unknown, needs source for Bosnia and Herzegovina).
