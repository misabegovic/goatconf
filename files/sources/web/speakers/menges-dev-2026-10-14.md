What I talk about
Monolith to event-driven platform
Why the split usually goes wrong, what "centralised writes, regional reads" costs you, and how to reason about eventual consistency without hand-waving.
Multi-region architecture
Serving users across continents: projections, replication lag, retries, partial failure, and the operational surface it all adds.
Deterministic tooling for AI-assisted development
What coding agents actually need in order to not wreck an architecture - structure before generation, grading after, and why that has to be parsers rather than another model.
Architecture as something you can query
Treating a codebase as a graph of facts: dependency cycles, layer violations, dead routes, and change impact you can put in front of a review.

Short bio
Dejan Menges is a staff engineer with more than twenty-five years spent designing distributed systems. He works on moving organisations from monoliths to scalable, event-driven architectures, and builds Enola, an open-source architecture intelligence tool for developers and coding agents.
Long bio
Dejan Menges has spent over twenty-five years building and operating distributed systems, most of that time on the unglamorous half of the problem: what happens to an architecture after the design document is approved. He currently works as a staff engineer at Vinted, where his focus has been taking a successful monolith and turning it into a platform that serves users across continents - centralised writes, regional read projections, and the eventual consistency, retries and partial failures that come with them.
He is the author and maintainer of Enola, an open-source tool that turns a codebase into a queryable architecture graph so that developers, coding agents and CI can catch architectural regressions before they are committed - using parsers and graph algorithms rather than model inference. He also writes augur, an open-source tool for finding what is hidden in a file, aimed at the instruction files coding agents load into context on every session. He co-founded FairwayHub, a social platform for golf communities now being wound down, and has contributed to open source since long before it was a hiring signal.
He writes about architecture at menges.dev and on the Vinted engineering blog, and speaks about event-driven systems, multi-region architecture, and what deterministic tooling can do for AI-assisted development.
