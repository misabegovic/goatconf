The organiser wrote, verbatim:

> Also, let's change the licence to be Apache 2.0 and put a copyright for the brand, goats and all GoatConf related. All rights reserved

The agent noted that the repository had been under the MIT licence since 2026-10-24 (sources/conversations/2026-10-24--org--foss-values-and-mit-licence.md); that the Apache License 2.0 grants no rights to the licensor's names and marks; that the photos, fonts, speakers' photos and supporters' logos belong to others and keep their licences; that the stickers and share cards are made to be shared; that copies already published under MIT stay MIT; and that the painted goats were made with an image generator, so copyright in them may be weak in some countries and the brand is the stronger protection. It also said it is not a lawyer.

Asked what "All rights reserved" should cover, the organiser chose "Brand and art (Recommended)": the GoatConf name, logo, coin and seal, the goat mascot and every painted goat, the stickers and the share cards, while the code, the site's text and the brain's wiki are under Apache 2.0. Asked what people may do with the stickers, goats and share cards, they chose "Share them unchanged (Recommended)": anyone may post, print and use them unchanged to talk about or promote GoatConf, and anything else needs the organiser's written permission.

Shown the LICENSE, a NOTICE file, a copyright line under every page, and the brand page's "Using it" section, they chose "Ship it (Recommended)".
