title: "Areas, named formats, and sessions or events: the review of what GoatConf invites"
date: 2026-10-19
kind: conversation
---

After the eight areas went live, the organiser asked, verbatim:

> Ok. Let's analyze the types we support and invite. Review from perspective of attendee and potential speaker

The agent reviewed the session formats, the areas, the kinds of events
people add and the audiences the home page names. Offered four small
fixes, the organiser picked three:

- Rename the proposal form's "Discipline" question to "Area", asking what
  the session is about rather than the speaker's own field, and ask in
  "Who is it for?" for the level too.
- Let a session whose format is "something else" show under its own
  name (for example Live coding or Fireside chat) in place of "Session".
- Say on the talk page and the add page which one to use: a session takes
  one of the hour slots at IPI Academy on Thursday or Friday and is picked
  by the organiser; an event is run and paid for by its host, on any day
  from 2 to 6 June.

Not picked: naming AI/ML and data people, researchers and leaders in the
home page's audience line and on the supporters page.

Left for separate decisions: letting a workshop run longer than one hour,
a lightning-talk hour, a way for attendees to find sessions by area, and
what the computer centres run (unknown, needs a source from IPI Academy).

While the form was being changed, the organiser asked, verbatim:

> Should we make it a multi select? As one proposal can span different things?

Offered "up to two, the first is the main one", "any number" and "keep
one", they picked "Any number": the form's Area question became
checkboxes ("What is the session about? Pick all that apply."), and a
record may list several areas, each shown as a tag. If the areas later
become tracks across venues, the track is picked by hand when the
program is built.

The form now reads, checked on its public page on 2026-10-19: "Who is it
for?" with the help text "Who should come, and at what level? For
example: anyone new to Rust, or founders before their first hire."; and
"Area", checkboxes, "What is the session about? Pick all that apply.",
with Engineering, AI/ML, Data, UX/UI, Product, Research, The business of
software, Leadership and Other.
