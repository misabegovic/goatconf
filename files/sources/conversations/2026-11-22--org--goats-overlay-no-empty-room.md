On 2026-11-22, after seeing the fix first built for the speakers' goats
(sources/conversations/2026-11-22--org--speaker-goats-on-the-left-and-smaller.md),
in which room was kept above every slot a goat stands on, so that lists
of open slots stood about 90 pixels apart and the cover grew by 57
pixels, the organiser wrote, verbatim:

> Why do we need empty spaces between? Can't the goats have overlay over the sections above?

Read as: no room is kept empty for a goat anywhere. A goat that stands on
the top edge of a slot or of a section stands at its own full size at its
own end, the right, and overlays whatever is above its seat: the section
above, the card above, a paragraph.

Where the rule this sets aside came from. That a goat never covers a
word, a link or a stamp was not the organiser's. It came from the
independent design review of the same day (its items 3 and 15, kept
outside the repository at ~/scratch/goatconf/shots/review15/REPORT.md)
and from the session building the site. It was written into the site on
the organiser's words about the closed food row
(sources/conversations/2026-11-22--org--meals-section-weird-when-collapsed.md),
which asked only that the rows hold still and work in both states. That
rule is what first moved goats to the left and drew them smaller, and
then, when the organiser asked why, kept room for them: 60 to 72 pixels
between the home page's sections
(sources/conversations/2026-11-22--org--section-goats-too-small-and-cut-off.md)
and the room above the speakers' cards. The organiser's words here
withdraw both the rule, for a goat on a top edge, and the room.

What still holds, and is not the organiser's to restate: a press reaches
the link or button a goat stands over; a goat does not cover the face in
a speaker's photo; closed rows hold still; a press on a goat does not
open or close a section; nothing inside a closed section is a stop; each
speaker keeps their own goat.

This is shown to the organiser before it is published.

The same day, about a goat drawn over a link, the organiser wrote,
verbatim:

> If a goat is drawn above a link, clicking on a goat should not trigger that link

Read as: a press that lands on the goat does the goat's trick and nothing
else. It follows no link, presses no button, opens or closes no section
and opens no card that lies under the goat, a whole card or slot that is
itself one link included. A press on the part of a link the goat does not
cover works as before. Only the empty corners of the goat's picture let a
press through.
